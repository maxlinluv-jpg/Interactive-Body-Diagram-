THE CENTRAL QUESTION

Traditional diet
vs. modern diet.

There is no single “traditional diet” or “modern diet.” This comparison looks at characteristics that can be measured across dietary patterns rather than labeling an entire category as healthy or unhealthy.

TRADITIONAL PATTERNS

Common characteristics

  • Often emphasize minimally processed or locally prepared foods.
  • May contain substantial amounts of legumes, whole grains, vegetables, fruit, nuts, seeds or fish depending on culture.
  • Food preparation and staple foods vary greatly by region and history.
  • Some traditional patterns can also be high in sodium, saturated fat, or refined carbohydrates depending on the foods and preparation used.
MODERN PATTERNS

Common characteristics

  • Often include more commercially packaged and convenience foods.
  • May contain foods and drinks with added sugars, sodium, or refined grains.
  • Portion sizes, availability, and food environments can differ substantially from historical patterns.
  • Modern diets can also include nutrient-dense whole foods; “modern” does not automatically mean unhealthy.

SCIENTIFIC CAUTION

Patterns matter more than labels.

The Dietary Guidelines for Americans emphasize healthy dietary patterns and a variety of nutrient-dense foods. Our project therefore treats “traditional” and “modern” as research categories, not as automatic health scores.
