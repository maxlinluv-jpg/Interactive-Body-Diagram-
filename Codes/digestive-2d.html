Important: The 30-second animation compresses major events for learning. It does not preserve real durations or imply that the same food physically travels from mouth to anus in 30 seconds. Whole-gut transit commonly takes roughly 1–3 days, sometimes longer.

Where nutrients go

Digestion breaks large food molecules into smaller absorbable units. Most sugars and amino acids cross the small-intestinal lining into blood capillaries and travel first to the liver through the hepatic portal circulation. Most long-chain dietary fats are packaged into chylomicrons and enter lymphatic lacteals before reaching the bloodstream. These pathways are simplified here.

Blood → liverMost absorbed sugars and amino acids enter portal blood and reach the liver first.
Lymph → bloodMost long-chain dietary fats enter intestinal lymph vessels before joining the blood.
Water recoveryThe colon absorbs remaining water and electrolytes; it does not recover every drop.

What changes along the way?

Chewing fragments food; saliva starts starch digestion; stomach acid and enzymes help digest proteins; bile disperses fat droplets; pancreatic and intestinal enzymes complete much of chemical digestion. Absorbable molecules cross the intestinal lining. Indigestible material and some fiber continue to the colon, where microbes ferment some fibers and water is recovered before stool is formed.

Educational visualization, not a diagnostic tool. Transit times vary with meal composition, hydration, activity, health, and individual physiology. Anatomy is illustrative rather than a patient-specific or clinical-grade model.
